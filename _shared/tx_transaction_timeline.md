# Texas Residential Transaction Timeline

Reference for 04_transaction_coordinator. Also useful to 02 (for CMA work), 03 (for client comms), and any agent who needs a refresher on Texas-specific transaction mechanics.

Texas residential real estate operates on TREC (Texas Real Estate Commission) promulgated forms. Not optional. Custom contracts require attorney involvement. The timelines below assume the standard TREC 1-4 Family Residential Contract (Resale).

---

## The standard buyer-side timeline

Day 0 = contract execution (both parties signed).

| Day | Milestone | Who's responsible |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Contract executed | Both parties |
| +1 to +3 | Option fee paid to seller, earnest money to title (within 3 business days) | Buyer |
| +1 to +5 | Inspection scheduled (during option period) | Buyer / buyer's agent |
| +5 to +10 | Option period ends (typical 5-10 days, set in contract) | Buyer |
| +5 to +10 | If terminating: written notice (TREC Termination of Contract) | Buyer |
| +7 to +14 | Lender appraisal ordered | Lender |
| +7 to +14 | Survey ordered (or T-47 affidavit from seller) | Title |
| +14 to +21 | Title commitment delivered | Title |
| +14 to +28 | HOA resale package delivered (if applicable) | Seller / HOA |
| +21 | Financing contingency expires (typical 21 days, set in contract) | Buyer |
| +21 | Appraisal contingency expires (often same as financing) | Buyer |
| +30 to +45 | Final loan approval / clear to close | Lender |
| Close - 3 business days | Closing Disclosure (CD) delivered to buyer | Lender via title |
| Close - 1 day | Final walkthrough | Buyer + buyer's agent |
| Close day | Closing + funding + possession | All |

Total typical timeline: **30–45 days** from execution to close. Faster is possible (cash deals can close in 14 days). Slower happens (lender delays, repair amendments, HOA delays).

## TREC forms you'll see

The full TREC form library is at trec.texas.gov. Forms most commonly used:

| Form | Used for |
|---|---|
| **TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale)** | The main contract for resale homes |
| **TREC New Home Contract (Completed Construction)** | New build, already complete |
| **TREC New Home Contract (Incomplete Construction)** | New build, still being built |
| **TREC Residential Condominium Contract (Resale)** | Resale condos |
| **TREC Farm and Ranch Contract** | Rural / acreage |
| **TREC Termination of Contract** | Option period termination |
| **TREC Buyer's Notice of Termination Pursuant to Contract Provisions** | Termination per a specific contract provision (financing, appraisal, etc.) |
| **TREC Notice of Buyer's Termination of Contract** | General termination notice |
| **TREC Amendment to Contract** | Any amendment (repair credit, extension, price change, etc.) |

## TXR addendums

Texas Realtors (TXR) publishes addendums that supplement TREC forms. Common ones:

| Addendum | When |
|---|---|
| **TXR-1406 — Seller's Disclosure Notice** | Almost always (required for most resale) |
| **TXR-1903 — Amendment to Contract** | Same as TREC Amendment, often used in practice |
| **TXR-1906 — Lead-Based Paint Addendum** | Homes built before 1978 |
| **TXR-1601 — Addendum for Property in HOA** | When property has an HOA |
| **TXR-1909 — Seller's Temporary Residential Lease** | When seller stays post-close (max 90 days) |
| **TXR-1910 — Buyer's Temporary Residential Lease** | When buyer moves in pre-close |
| **TXR-1908 — Pre-Approval Letter** | (sometimes — informal in practice) |

## Critical Texas-specific concepts

### Option period

**This is Texas-unique.** During the option period, the buyer can terminate the contract for ANY reason and recover their earnest money (minus the option fee, which is non-refundable to seller). Typical option period: 5–10 days. Typical option fee: $100–500.

The buyer must give **written notice of termination** before the option period expires. The deadline is precise — "11:59 PM" not "end of business."

Option fee is paid to the seller (not held in escrow). It's the seller's "consideration" for granting the buyer the right to walk.

### Financing contingency

Typical 21 days from execution. If the buyer's loan is denied, they can terminate without penalty. Written notice required, usually with denial letter from lender.

### Appraisal contingency

Typical 21 days from execution. If the appraisal comes in below contract price, buyer can:
- Terminate
- Renegotiate price
- Bring cash to cover the gap
- Have the seller credit the difference

This is one of the most common "real" risks in a deal. Plan for it.

### Earnest money

Earned by the buyer's commitment. Typically 1% of purchase price. Goes to title within 3 business days of contract execution. Held in escrow until close.

At close: applied to buyer's funds owed.
At termination during option period: refunded to buyer.
At termination outside option period: depends on which contingency was used; sometimes forfeited to seller.

### Seller's Disclosure Notice (TXR-1406)

Required for most residential resale transactions. Seller fills it out. Buyer reviews. Material defects must be disclosed. If a seller knew about an issue and didn't disclose, that can be cause for legal action post-close.

### Mineral rights

TREC contracts include a section for retained vs. transferred mineral rights. In urban Austin, most homeowners don't have mineral rights to retain or transfer (the original developer retained them generations ago). Worth verifying in the contract; rarely a sticking point.

### Survey

Buyer typically requires a survey or accepts the seller's existing survey + T-47 affidavit. T-47 = seller's sworn statement that no changes have been made to the property boundaries since the prior survey. New survey costs $400–700.

### HOA resale package

If property is in an HOA, the HOA must provide a resale certificate plus bylaws, financials, recent meeting minutes. Costs $200–500 (typically seller's responsibility). 10–14 day turnaround is common; can be slow.

## TRID — federal Closing Disclosure rule

(Not Texas-specific, but enforced equally.)

The Closing Disclosure (CD) must be delivered to the buyer at least **3 business days before close**. Federal rule (TRID — TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure).

Changes that **reset the 3-business-day clock**:
- APR change beyond tolerance
- Loan product change (e.g. fixed-rate to ARM)
- Prepayment penalty added

Changes that **do NOT reset** the clock:
- Adjustment to closing costs within tolerance
- Date change for close

If the lender hasn't issued the CD 3 business days before scheduled close, you have a problem. Push close, don't try to muscle through.

## Wire fraud — the constant background threat

Wire fraud in real estate is rampant. Scammers intercept buyer-seller-title-lender email threads and send fake wire instructions. The standard precautions:

1. **Title sends wire instructions only through their secure channel** (their portal, or a sealed letter, or in-person).
2. **Buyer verifies wire instructions by phone** before sending. Call the title contact's known phone number — not the number on the wire instructions.
3. **All client comms about wire instructions** (drafted by 03) include the explicit warning: "Do not act on wire instructions from any source other than [title contact name] at [title company]. Verify by phone before sending."

If wire fraud is even suspected, escalate to Diana immediately.

## Closing day

What happens:
- Final walkthrough (morning of, typically)
- Closing happens at title company (or remote / hybrid)
- Buyer signs loan docs, deed, ID verification, etc.
- Seller signs deed transfer, payoff, etc.
- Title funds the transaction (wires loan proceeds, disburses seller proceeds, records the deed)
- Possession typically immediate at funding (per contract; sometimes delayed by seller temp lease)

Wires take 1–4 hours to settle. Recording of the deed at the Travis County Clerk's office happens electronically and is typically completed same-day.

After funding:
- Title sends final documents to all parties
- Lender sends servicing transfer notification
- Buyer / agent / coordinator update utility transfers (Austin Energy, Austin Water, gas, internet)

## Property-tax escrow estimate

For a Travis County buyer:

```
Monthly tax escrow ≈ (assessed value × 0.020) / 12
```

Where 0.020 is the combined Travis County rate (school + city + county + healthcare + ACC, plus any MUD). Range is 1.9%–2.2% depending on the property's specific jurisdictions.

For a $700,000 home → $14,000/year ÷ 12 → $1,167/month.

Lenders typically escrow taxes monthly. Buyer should plan for this on top of P&I and insurance.

## When timelines slip

The deal isn't dead. Most slips can be handled. The pattern is:

1. 04 flags the slip 48+ hours before it materializes
2. 04 bullets the facts to 03
3. 03 drafts the client comm in the agent's voice
4. The agent reviews and sends
5. The fix is documented: amendment, extension, repair credit, etc.
6. Updated dates flow into the tracker

The deals that fall apart usually fall apart not because of the slip itself, but because the slip wasn't surfaced and the client felt blindsided. Surface, surface, surface.
