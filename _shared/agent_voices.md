# Agent Voices

The voice cards for each member of Diana Lin Realty. Used by 03_client_communication to draft in the right person's voice. **Update this file** when you notice an agent consistently editing drafts in a particular direction — that's voice drift the cards should capture.

When you replace a team member or hire a new one, replace the voice card here. The names in this starter version are illustrative (Maria, Tom, Jordan) — Diana, swap them as needed for the actual team.

---

## Diana Lin (owner)

**Tag:** `human:Diana`
**Specialty:** Seller-side relocations, high-touch past-client work, anything over $1.2M, anything legal-adjacent.

### Voice characteristics

- **Warm but professional.** She sounds like a friend who happens to be very good at her job. Not overly casual, not stuffy.
- **Opens with warmth.** Often a brief personal acknowledgment: "Sarah — hope the move-in went smoothly," or "Thanks for thinking of us." (She's the only agent who routinely opens this way.)
- **Long-relationship references.** She remembers details from past deals and references them — "Last time we worked together, the closing went so fast you barely had time to pack."
- **Sentence pattern:** Medium-length, complete sentences. Doesn't fragment for effect.
- **Emoji use:** Very rare. Maybe a smiley face once a quarter.
- **Email opening:** "Hi <Name>" or "<Name> —"
- **Email closing:** Always "Diana" (not "Diana Lin"), followed by her full signature block.
- **Sign-off line:** "Best, Diana" or just "Diana"
- **Texts:** Sparing. Diana mostly emails. When she texts, it's short and warm: "Talked to Brittany — earnest money landed. We're good. — D"
- **Phone call follow-ups:** Diana frequently follows up a phone conversation with a written summary ("Just to put it in writing what we discussed...").

### Phrases she uses

- "Glad we got there." (After a deal closes)
- "Let's get on a quick call — I'd rather talk this through."
- "You're in good hands with [team member]."
- "Thank you for your patience on this."
- "I'm not the right person to advise on [X] — let me connect you with [Y]." (When she's at the edge of her lane)

### Phrases she never uses

- "Synergy," "win-win," "let's table that"
- Real-estate jargon when plain English works
- Anything that sounds like a script

### Signature block

```
Diana Lin
Diana Lin Realty
(512) 555-0170
diana@dianalinrealty.example
www.dianalinrealty.example
```

### What she draws the line at

- Drafts on her behalf for past clients she's known >5 years (she'll always write those personally — flag back to her)
- Anything legal-adjacent (she'll draft herself or with counsel)
- Anything to a referrer (she always thanks them personally)

## Maria Reyes (senior agent, 5 years)

**Tag:** `human:Maria`
**Specialty:** Mid-market central Austin ($500k–$1.2M), buyers and sellers, mixed.

### Voice characteristics

- **Direct, warm, slightly more casual than Diana.** Maria sounds like a competent friend, not a service provider.
- **Opens without filler.** "Pat —" or "Pat, Jamie —" (with no greeting beneath). Gets to the point in the first sentence.
- **Sentence pattern:** Mixed lengths. Short sentences for emphasis. Longer ones when laying out options.
- **Uses lists** to lay out options ("Three options...", "Two things to think about..."). Almost always offers a structured choice rather than asking open-ended.
- **Emoji use:** One per email maximum, only when it fits naturally (🎉 on offer-accepted, 🐕 in a casual reference to a client's dog). Texts can use more.
- **Email opening:** "<Name> —" or "<Name>, <Name> —" for couples. Skips "Hi" or "Hello."
- **Email closing:** "Maria" followed by signature block.
- **Texts:** Frequent, short, casual. Often signs with "— Maria" or "— M". Uses emojis more in texts.
- **Channel choice:** Defaults to email for substance, text for logistics. Will explicitly suggest a phone call when a decision is needed.

### Phrases she uses

- "Quick recap of what we discussed..."
- "Want to jump on a 10-minute call?"
- "My read on this is..."
- "Honestly, I'd lean toward [option]."
- "No pressure to decide today, but..."
- "Heads up: ..."
- "I'll confirm by EOD."
- "Wear shoes you can walk in." (Maria's tour-day tell)

### Phrases she never uses

- "Hand in hand," "dream home," "this beauty won't last"
- "Just touching base," "circling back"
- "Hope this finds you well"

### Signature block

```
Maria Reyes
Diana Lin Realty
(512) 555-0184
maria@dianalinrealty.example
```

### What she draws the line at

- Pricing recommendations she hasn't approved (always flag for her review)
- Anything to her past clients (she likes those personal — flag for her draft)
- Wire instructions (always title, always with warning)

## Tom Buchanan (senior agent, 5 years)

**Tag:** `human:Tom`
**Specialty:** Condos, townhomes, smaller properties, investor-adjacent (small landlords). East Austin focus.

### Voice characteristics

- **Direct, telegraphic, no softening.** Tom doesn't sugar-coat. His clients know what they're getting.
- **Texts are very short.** Often 1-2 sentences. Starts with the recipient's first name.
- **Emails are short too.** Tom doesn't write long emails. He writes 3-5 line emails.
- **Uses "FYI" frequently.** "FYI — listing came in at $618k. Below comp. Worth a look."
- **Emoji use:** None. Never.
- **Email opening:** "<Name>," or just dives in.
- **Email closing:** Just "Tom" or his initials "T.B." No signature block on texts, full one on emails.
- **Channel choice:** Defaults to text for almost everything. Email only when something needs to be in writing for the record (offers, counters, contract dates).
- **Tone:** Matter-of-fact. If something's bad news, he says it straight. If something's good news, he says that straight too.

### Phrases he uses

- "FYI"
- "Heads up"
- "Quick call?"
- "Decide by [time] please."
- "I'd pass on this one."
- "Worth a look."
- "Numbers don't work."

### Phrases he never uses

- Anything warm-and-fuzzy
- Long preambles
- "I hope" or "I just"
- Sales-y language

### Signature block (email only)

```
Tom Buchanan
Diana Lin Realty

```

### What he draws the line at

- Anything emotional with a client (he'd rather Diana take that)
- Long-form client comfort communications (passes to Maria or Diana)
- Posting on social media (Jordan does that for the team)

## Jordan Park (ramping agent, 6 months in)

**Tag:** `human:Jordan`
**Specialty:** First-time buyers (good entry-level deals), nurture sequences, social media. Picks up "warm but not urgent" leads as she ramps.

### Voice characteristics

- **Warm but not yet confident.** Jordan's voice is still developing. Her drafts tend toward longer than they need to be — when in doubt, edit toward shorter.
- **Uses "we" instead of "I"** when she hasn't built a relationship yet. ("We can pull together a brief for you.") As she gains confidence, she'll shift to "I."
- **Loves a local detail.** She'll work in one Austin-specific micro-detail per email when it fits (a neighborhood thing, a coffee shop reference). It signals she knows Austin.
- **Sentence pattern:** Medium length, sometimes wordier than needed. The voice card recommends drafting tight and trusting Jordan to know when to add warmth.
- **Emoji use:** Light. One per email when it fits the tone, more in texts.
- **Email opening:** "Hi <Name>," (more conventional than Maria's dash style — Jordan hasn't settled into a signature opening yet).
- **Email closing:** "Jordan" or "Jordan / Diana Lin Realty" depending on formality.
- **Channel choice:** Balanced. Email for first contact, text for follow-up.

### Phrases she uses

- "Just wanted to check in with you..."
- "Hope your week is going well!"
- "Let me know what works for you."
- "Quick thought: ..."

### Phrases she's working to drop (in the editing loop with Diana)

- "Just wanted to" (too softening)

## How to use this file (for 03_client_communication)

1. Look up the agent assigned to the case (the `assigned_agent` field in case.md frontmatter).
2. Read that agent's voice card before drafting.
3. **Also read the prior 3+ entries in this case's `communication_log.md`** — the voice card is the baseline, but the case-specific voice drift matters more.
4. Draft.
5. After the agent sends, if they edited the draft, log the edit type and consider whether the voice card needs updating.

## When to add a voice card

When Diana hires a new team member, add a card here on their first day. Initial card can be sparse — observe the new hire's writing for the first month and fill out the card from actual examples. Don't fabricate a voice; build the card from their real comms.

## When to remove a voice card

If a team member leaves, archive their card in `_shared/_archived_voices/` rather than deleting outright. Their old comms still exist in past case files; the card explains them.
